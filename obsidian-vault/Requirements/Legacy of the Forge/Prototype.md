#### Prototype Priorities

The following systems form the minimum viable playable experience:

1. Crafting and item creation.
    
2. Persistent customers and equipment ownership.
    
3. Time progression and customer activities.
    
4. Event generation and resolution.
    
5. Equipment durability and repairs.
    
6. Item history and basic legacy.
    
7. Customer returns and discovery of item stories.
    

Aura, complex rumour networks, inheritance, multiple display locations, and advanced social interactions are valuable extensions, but should not block the core prototype.

#### Prototype Acceptance Criteria

The prototype should be considered successful when the following scenario can be completed:

1. The player receives a commission from a customer.
    
2. The player crafts an item using available materials.
    
3. The item is delivered and recorded in the customer's inventory.
    
4. Time advances and the customer uses the item in an appropriate activity.
    
5. A world event occurs involving that customer and item.
    
6. The event changes the item's durability and records its participation in the item's history.
    
7. The customer eventually returns to the forge.
    
8. The player learns what happened to the item through the customer's account.
    
9. The player repairs the item or chooses to replace it.
    
10. The item's history remains intact regardless of the player's decision.
    

**The essential demonstration:** An ordinary item created by the player becomes a unique object with a history because of what happens to it in the world.

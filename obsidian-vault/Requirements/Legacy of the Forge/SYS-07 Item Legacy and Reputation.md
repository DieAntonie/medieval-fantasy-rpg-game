**Purpose:** Give individual items a persistent identity and history that can outlive their usefulness or original owners.

Legacy must be distinct from an item's physical condition. A damaged sword may be historically significant, while a newly crafted sword may be physically superior but entirely unknown.

#### Core requirements

- **REQ-07.01** Each item must maintain a persistent legacy record.
    
- **REQ-07.02** Significant events must be capable of contributing to an item's legacy.
    
- **REQ-07.03** Legacy must not increase simply through repetitive use; meaningful events should drive its development.
    
- **REQ-07.04** An item's legacy must persist independently of its durability.
    
- **REQ-07.05** An item's legacy must persist through ownership changes.
    
- **REQ-07.06** Legacy must be capable of developing in different dimensions:
    
    - Renown: How widely the item is known.
        
    - Prestige: How much respect or admiration it commands.
        
    - Myth: How much folklore, superstition, or perceived supernatural significance surrounds it.
        
- **REQ-07.07** Legacy must be capable of both positive and negative development.
    
- **REQ-07.08** The player must be able to discover an item's legacy through interactions, returning customers, rumours, or records.
    

#### Extended requirements

- **REQ-07.09** Items may develop individual names or titles based on their history.
    
- **REQ-07.10** Different communities may interpret the same item's history differently.
    
- **REQ-07.11** An item's legacy may influence how future customers perceive or value it.
    
- **REQ-07.12** An item may become historically significant even after it is no longer usable.
    

#### UI requirements

- UI-S07.01 The player must be able to inspect an item's known legacy.
    
- UI-S07.02 The interface must distinguish between physical condition and historical significance.
    
- UI-S07.03 The player must be able to view significant events contributing to an item's legacy.
    
- UI-S07.04 The interface must distinguish between confirmed history and uncertain information.
